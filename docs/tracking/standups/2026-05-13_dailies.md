### Exploration of alternatives

Drafted 5 alternative on-network optimization paths (A-E):
- A: Cell-as-Box with adaptive storage (cell API uniform; internal storage adapts to no-speculation hot path)
- B: Threshold-as-cell-property (derived value on read; eliminates propagator-as-decoration)
- C: Propagator-fire skip for monotone counters (cell declares `:fires-on '(threshold-crossing)`)
- D: Hot/warm/cold cells (extends BSP-LE Track 0's hot/cold split to cells)
- E: BSP-aware piggyback (threshold check in scheduler drain pass)

### User's architectural insight

User articulated the load-bearing principle: "Cell / Propagator / Scheduler Orthogonality" — the three layers must remain orthogonal. CALM theorem says monotone computation on fixed topology converges regardless of evaluation order — the order is the scheduler's concern; the computation is the cell+propagator's concern.

User specifically called out: "The problem I see, is about coupling too much on the scheduler, complecting what should be orthogonal — making a thin scheduler and portability more tricky."

This rejected Option E (couples to BSP scheduler; Gauss-Seidel and future Zig-LLVM schedulers have no equivalent drain pass; loses portability) and validated Options A+B+C+D (all scheduler-neutral; live at cell/propagator layer).

### Codification

Codified Cell/Propagator/Scheduler Orthogonality as a top-level principle in DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.org. The principle establishes:
1. Three orthogonal layers (Cell, Propagator, Scheduler)
2. Optimization location rule (cell-level storage; propagator-level fire-pattern; scheduler-level efficiency only)
3. Anti-pattern: scheduler-coupled optimization (Option E was the example)
4. Portability implication (Prologos networks should be runnable across language boundaries — Racket prototype, Zig+LLVM lowering, future self-hosted runtime)
5. Multiple-scheduler invariance (Gauss-Seidel, BSP, Zig-LLVM all run same network with same semantics)
6. Distributed-concurrent runtime belongs at scheduler+networking layer, NOT propagator network itself

### Spike methodology refinements applied mid-run

Initial measurement showed W1 ≈ 2 ns/call, raising a concern about JIT dead-code elimination. Two refinements applied for honesty:

1. **Added W1+ measurement with dispatch overhead** (~290 lines total file): cell-meta lookup via `vector-ref` + tier check + under-speculation check before entering the fast path. This represents the realistic upper bound for the production implementation. Result: 6.4–7.0 ns/call — still well within target.

2. **Defeated dead-code elimination in baseline (B.M7)**: captured `struct-copy` results in mutable boxes; added a nested struct-copy variant mirroring the real M7 pattern (`prop-network → prop-net-hot`). Result: B.M7.2 = 5.7–5.9 ns/call (vs Pre-0 M7's 24 ns/call). The gap is explained by simpler mock structs (mock-network has 3 fields vs real prop-network's 3 fields wrapping prop-net-hot with multiple fields) — not by elimination. The W1 measurement of 2.1 ns is robust (verified via W2 crossing-count: exactly 50100 = 50000 measurement + 100 warmup, proving the mutation IS happening).

### Critical observations

1. **The W3 zero-GC result is structurally robust**: direct fixnum mutation on a struct's mutable field allocates ZERO bytes. The 1.1 KB allocation observed across 100k operations is from the loop machinery (`in-range` iterators, etc.), not from the cell-write itself. This eliminates the entire empirical foundation of the D.3 hybrid pivot.

- BSP scheduler writes cell at round exit OR immediately on exhaustion

The cell IS canonical at every observation point. The local-var is just the scheduler's working register; mid-round staleness is unobservable (Phase 3C consumers fire AT round boundaries, where the cell is current).
